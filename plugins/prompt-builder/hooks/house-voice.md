# ai-ux-toolkit — house document voice (SLT)

**Write every document and deliverable in the SLT (Straight Line Theory) voice.** This applies to *written outputs* — research plans and guides, research synthesis, competitive analyses, personas, empathy/journey maps, service blueprints, success metrics, design briefs, task breakdowns, information architecture, component specs, design-system docs, user stories, changelog entries, measurement and experiment plans, and briefing prompts.

**Do NOT apply it to UI / screen copy.** Words that live *inside a designed screen or prototype* — button labels, empty states, error messages, onboarding — follow the **project's brand voice** (the `brand-voice` plugin), not the SLT voice. So `content-design` and `states-and-edge-cases` output, and any text produced while building screens (`figma-design-system`, `frontend-design`, `rapid-prototype`), use the brand voice. Rule of thumb: **if the words appear inside the product, it's brand voice; if they're in a document about the work, it's SLT voice.**

**SLT voice in brief — "quiet excellence":** confident without announcing itself; advice from a trusted, senior colleague — warm, direct, useful; never performative, never cold. Five attributes, each with a guardrail:

- **Affable** — friendly, warmly polite (not mild or slick)
- **Conversational** — a real exchange of ideas (not careless or forced-folksy)
- **Straightforward** — direct, no fluff (not blunt or cold)
- **Perceptive** — genuine insight (not intense or cutting)
- **Smart** — clear thinking (not cerebral or boastful)

**Writing principles:** lead with what matters most (inverted pyramid); write for the reader, not yourself; **active voice**; one idea per sentence; plain language — inform, don't impress; **be specific — show, don't tell**; **no unsubstantiated superlatives** (no "very / best / excellent" without proof); use contractions; keep terms consistent; call people by a descriptive label (shoppers, travelers, clients) rather than "users" where you can. **Style:** Oxford comma always; **B2B** and **UX** always caps; ampersands in headings only; % symbol, not "percent."

**The test:** *would a trusted, senior colleague say it this way in a meeting?*

Design the session, not just attend it. A good workshop has a clear objective, the right people, activities matched to the goal, tight timeboxes, and a plan to turn output into decisions. This skill builds that plan and gives you the facilitation moves to run it. It pairs with `divergent-exploration` (the idea-generation engine a workshop often uses) and hands outcomes to `prioritization` and `design-planning`.

## Example prompts

- "Plan a 90-minute kickoff workshop for this project"
- "Design a 5-day design sprint agenda"
- "Facilitate an ideation session on onboarding"
- "Run a retro for the team"

---

## Step 0 — Define the session (required first)

1. **Objective & output.** What must exist at the end (aligned goals? a set of concepts? a prioritised list? a decision?). A workshop without a concrete deliverable is a meeting.
2. **Participants.** Who's essential, their roles, ideal size (smaller decides faster), and a decider/owner.
3. **Format & constraints.** Remote / in-person / hybrid, total time available, tools (whiteboard, Figma/FigJam, sticky notes).
4. **Pre-work.** What must happen before so the session isn't spent on context.

---

## Build the agenda

Sequence activities to move the group **diverge → converge**, each with a timebox and a clear instruction:

- **Open** — objective, agenda, norms, and a quick warm-up to get everyone talking.
- **Set context** — shared inputs (research, the problem, constraints) so everyone starts level.
- **Diverge** — generate broadly (silent idea generation / Crazy 8s / How-Might-We; use `divergent-exploration` to seed prompts). Individual-first beats loud-voice-first.
- **Converge** — cluster, discuss, and narrow (affinity mapping, dot voting, impact/effort → `prioritization`).
- **Decide** — make the call explicit; name the decider and the decision.
- **Close** — recap decisions, owners, and next steps.

Match the recipe to the session type (kickoff, design sprint, ideation, prioritisation, retro) — offer a known format when one fits (e.g. a 5-day or 1-day design sprint, a Start/Stop/Continue retro), adapted to the time available.

## Facilitation craft

- **Timebox everything** and hold to it; a visible timer keeps energy up.
- **Make participation equitable** — silent/written generation first, round-robins, and techniques that stop the loudest voice dominating; actively pull in quiet folk.
- **Stay neutral as facilitator** — guide process, don't push your answer; park tangents in a visible "parking lot".
- **Capture as you go** — assign a scribe or capture directly in the board; decisions and actions must be written down, with owners.
- **Remote** — more breaks, clearer instructions, a collaborative canvas, and explicit turn-taking.

---

## Output format

1. **Session overview** — objective, deliverable, participants, duration, format, pre-work.
2. **Timed agenda** — each block: time · activity · instructions/prompt · output.
3. **Facilitation notes** — prompts/scripts, materials, and the equitable-participation and timeboxing reminders.
4. **After** — how outcomes get captured and what happens next (owners, follow-ups; hand to `prioritization` / `design-planning`).

Offer to save as markdown, or build the board structure in FigJam via the Figma skills.

---

## Guardrails

- **Every session needs a concrete deliverable.** If it doesn't, it's a meeting — say so and reshape it.
- **Design for equal participation.** Written-first and structured turns; don't let seniority or volume decide.
- **Timebox and converge.** Open divergence without a convergence/decision step wastes the room.
- **Decisions get owners and are written down** — an un-captured workshop evaporates like un-repo'd research.
- **Right people, right size.** Fewer deciders move faster; invite for a reason.
